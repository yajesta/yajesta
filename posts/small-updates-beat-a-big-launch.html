Small updates beat a big launch

17 Nov 2026

I make games with friends. The one people have played the most is Become a Trillionaire. It has about 3.47 million visits, about 40,000 favourites, and a peak of around 5,200 people in the game at once. That did not arrive in a single launch week.

It is tempting to hold updates back so the first week feels packed. You get one busy week, then a thin one, because you have already spent the work you were sitting on. The players who arrived on launch day then meet a game that has gone quiet.

A small update every week is a reason for people who already play to come back. It does not need to be a big announcement. A fix, a new thing to do, or a change they can feel is enough. What they notice over time is that the game is still being looked after.

Launch day is useful because more people can find the game. It is not a finish line. The week after is the real test: do people still have a reason to return once the noise has stopped?

You do not need each update to be large. You need it to ship. A modest change that lands on time beats a bigger one that slips a month because you were saving it for something more complete.

If a feature is going to miss the week, ship a smaller piece of it. A partial change that players can use is better than a quiet week while you wait for the full version. The rest becomes next week's update, not a reason to skip.

When you fall behind, do not fold three weeks of work into one large drop. That recreates the same problem: a spike, then a gap. Get back to a smaller weekly ship as soon as you can.

Keep a simple list of what went out each week. After a few months you can see the game in that list, not only in the memory of launch day. It also makes it obvious when you have started to slip.

Clear The Snow is a newer team game. It is not in the same place as Become a Trillionaire. The useful move, while it is still early, is to start the weekly habit now rather than hold work back for a later splash.
