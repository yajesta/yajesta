Small updates beat a big launch

17 Nov 2026

I make games with friends. The one people have played the most is Become a Trillionaire. It has about 3.47 million visits and about 40,000 favourites, and at its busiest there were around 5,200 people in the game at once. Those numbers are nice to see. They did not come from one big day.

It is easy to treat a launch as the main event. You hold updates back so the first week feels big. You spend a lot on ads in a short burst, and you hope that first week does most of the work. I have found the opposite is true. The work that actually moved the game was the work we kept doing: a small update every week, and a steady amount of ads, before we launched and after.

A big launch is one moment. It looks like a finish line. In practice it is just the point where more people can find the game. If you have saved all of the work for that moment, you have a busy week and then a quiet one. If you keep going, the launch is just one week among many.

That is why a small update every week beat any single launch we could have planned. A weekly update is not a big announcement. It is a reason for people who already play to come back, and a sign that the game is still being looked after. You do not need each update to be large. You need it to happen, again and again. Over time that is what people feel: not one splash, but a game that is still moving.

Ads worked the same way. I ran a steady 20 to 40 ad credits a day, and I kept that up after we launched instead of spending a lot in one go and then stopping. A burst looks exciting. It can also leave you with nothing the following week. A steady day of ads is less dramatic, but it means people can still find the game on an ordinary Tuesday, including the people who would have missed a short burst.

The ads themselves got better because we kept running them. For a while the click-through rate sat at about 3 to 6 percent. Later it got to around 12 percent. I do not think that came from one clever change on one day. It came from treating the ads as something you keep doing, and keep improving, rather than something you only switch on for a launch.

I am not saying launches do not matter. They do. I am saying they matter less than the habit around them. If the habit is “save it up, then push”, you get a spike and a drop. If the habit is “a little, every week”, the game has a chance to grow when you are not looking at a launch date.

Clear The Snow is a newer team game. It is not in the same place as Become a Trillionaire, and I will not pretend otherwise. I mention it only because this is a simple lesson, and I would rather use it early than wait to learn it a second time.

I like to keep things simple, and to get the details right. This is one of those details. Do a little, every week. Keep the ads on. Do not ask a launch to do the work that only steady work can do.
